The Company Benefits application uses following terms:

Benefits


When a person is employed by a company in the United States, the company remunerates the employee with wages, several types of insurance, and a long-term savings plan (typically used for retirement). Benefits are remunerations other than wages.

Username


Unique identification for each user enrolled in the Benefits system. To access the current benefit selections for a specific employee, specify the employee Username to match the Username stored in the Benefits database.

Pre-tax


The costs of some benefits can be deducted from an employee's wages before income taxes are calculated. Selecting this option for a benefit can reduce the amount of tax that an individual pays to the government.

Post-tax


The costs of most benefits are deducted from an employee's wages after income taxes are calculated. Selecting this option can increase an employee's income tax; however, it might have other advantages.

Insurance


An economic service that limits the costs associated with a specific type of event or object, or that produces income if an unfortunate event occurs. For example, in the United States, an employee can purchase medical and dental insurance to limit costs associated with regular examinations, and to diagnose and treat illnesses and injuries.

Typos


Typographical errors. A typographical error is a spelling error.

Database


A repository for organizing and storing data and their interrelationships while controlling redundancy.

Paycheck


Payment of employee wages. In addition to the money paid, employers generally include a report showing how each benefit affects the amount of the paycheck.

Medical, Medical Plan


Medical insurance. Controls employee costs incurred from medical examinations, diagnoses, and treatments of an employe and his or her dependents. The Company Benefits Management System offers several plans that differ in their costs and advantages.

Life Insurance


Reimburses the beneficiary of the policy holder in the event of the death of the employee.

Maximum Coverage


Highest level of life or disability insurance coverage that an employee can select.

Minimum Coverage


Lowest level of life or disability insurance coverage that an employee can select.

Coverage


Specifies the expenses that an insurance company pays on the behalf of a policy holder. Coverage also identifies the conditions under which payments are made.

Deduction


Amount of money deducted from a paycheck to pay for an individual benefit or for a group of benefits of an employee.

Disability Insurance


Provides an employee with income if the employee becomes disabled and unable to work. Disability insurance pays a percentage of the employee's salary.

Disability Amount


Specifies the annual income level paid to an individual who becomes disabled.

Retirement


Long-term savings or investment program. The employee sets aside a portion of his or her earnings to use at a later time in life. Typically, the employee uses these contributions after retiring from the work force or after reaching retirement age (about age 65).

Savings Fund


An investment program for the retirement contributions. Often, an investment management firm will manage the contributions from all employees to provide stability and growth for the employee investments.

Salary


Annual wages for an employee.

Perks


Short for perquisite (a privilege, gain, or profit incidental to regular salary or wages). Small benefits that the employer offers to employees that are not salary, insurance, or retirement. In many cases, perks are items that help employee morale. In this sample, the perks are free beverages, a health club membership, and the use of a company car.
